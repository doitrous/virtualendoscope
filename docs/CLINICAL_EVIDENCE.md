# Clinical content evidence

> Recovered planning/research snapshot from the earlier unpublished work. Statements about missing assets, planned architecture and media exclusion describe that baseline. For current implementation and remaining limits, see [the current plan](UPPER-GI-WORLD-PLAN.md), [clinical audit](CLINICAL-SOURCES.md), [published atlas rights](CLINICAL-MEDIA.md) and [release evidence](RELEASE-READINESS.md).


Source review date: **13 October 2026**. This is an educational prototype. The material has not undergone medical validation, curriculum validation or clinical performance validation. Its checklists and quiz scores do not certify procedural competence, equipment reprocessing, sedation competence or patient readiness.

The primary practical reference is BSG/AUGIS **2026 best-practice upper GI endoscopy guidance**, accepted 20 February 2026 (DOI: [10.1136/flgastro-2025-103455](https://doi.org/10.1136/flgastro-2025-103455)). Explicit quality benchmarks come from [ESGE 2025 performance measures](https://www.esge.com/assets/downloads/pdfs/guidelines/2025_a-2674-4912.pdf). The content model lists source titles, organizations, publication years and URLs beside lessons and checklist steps in `training/content/curriculum.mjs`.

## Decisions reflected in the teaching

- Diagnostic EGD preparation uses hand hygiene and exposure-appropriate PPE. Routine surgical scrubbing or full sterile operating-room attire is not a required diagnostic EGD step. CDC differentiates routine hand hygiene from surgical hand antisepsis; ASGE unit-safety guidance supports standard precautions.
- Fasting numbers are scoped to healthy adults having elective anesthesia/procedural sedation. Actual intake, emergencies and delayed emptying require assessment. The source is the ASA 2017 baseline; the 2023 modular update was located but not fully retrieved and is not presented here as a separately verified source.
- BSG/ESGE 2021 classifies diagnostic endoscopy with biopsy as low bleeding risk. Medication teaching requires a documented plan rather than universal withholding. ASA-led 2024 GLP-1 guidance replaced blanket withholding with individualized risk assessment for most patients.
- Sedation teaching names classes and examples but supplies no doses. Choice, titration, monitoring, rescue and discharge depend on patient, procedure, training and local policy. BSG 2023 sedation guidance is the monitoring/recovery reference, alongside ASGE 2018.
- The landmark route and simulator stop rules are educational translations of the guidance. They do not model tissue force, realistic airway physiology, pharmacokinetics or patient-specific anatomical variation.
- ESGE's diagnostic examination time and photography standards are identified as guideline benchmarks. Reaching a landmark or allowing a clock to run does not prove adequate examination.
- Sampling is indication-specific. ESGE 2021 tissue sampling and BSG/AUGIS/RCPath 2025 biopsy guidance inform examples; BSG 2026 incorporates the newer UK recommendations. Histology, lesion resectability and local protocol can change the plan.

## Retrieval and content-review limits

Official BSG, ESGE, ASA, CDC and NIDDK pages/PDFs were reviewed. The ASGE 2017 curriculum was read in full through a Duke-hosted copy of the original ASGE publication; its authorship and DOI are retained. Some ASGE original pages/PDFs and the 2025 biopsy full text returned access errors; relevant publisher/society search records and the BSG 2026 text supported the limited included claims. Older ASGE equipment reviews explain stable instrument concepts, not a current purchasing comparison. Scope specifications, reprocessing cycles and valve behaviors must follow the actual device instructions.

The 2026 ASGE mucosal sampling clinical practice update was identified as a public-comment item; no draft recommendations are used. Guidance varies across jurisdictions, devices and resources and can change. Publication years refer to underlying guidance, not necessarily the current web-page update date. A qualified endoscopist and local sedation/infection-prevention leads should review clinical content before use in an organized training program. The simulator's interface and scoring are authored implementation choices rather than society-endorsed tools.
